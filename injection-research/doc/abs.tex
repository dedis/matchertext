\begin{abstract}
Injection remains one of the most persistent classes of software
vulnerability, and the \emph{matchertext} discipline has been proposed as a
structural defense: by requiring that the ASCII matcher pairs \verb|()|,
\verb|[]|, and \verb|{}| always match, untrusted input delimited by a matcher
pair cannot break out of its slot without producing an unmatched matcher.
This paper measures how much of the real injection landscape that proposal could
structurally reach.
We assemble a reproducible corpus from thirteen public vulnerability and
proof-of-concept (PoC) sources, anchored on the MITRE CVE List and enriched
with NVD, CISA Vulnrichment, KEV, EPSS, CWE, GitHub/Red~Hat/Debian advisories,
and four PoC databases (Exploit-DB, Nuclei, Metasploit, and PoC-in-GitHub).
From roughly 348k published CVEs we identify \textbf{91{,}760} injection
vulnerabilities and classify each along two axes (weakness family and
injection \emph{syntax} type) using a layered CWE-hierarchy, phrase-rule,
and naive-Bayes classifier.
For the subset carrying an extractable PoC payload, we reduce each attack to a
\emph{syntactic skeleton} and group vulnerabilities by structural shape.
Finally, we test each skeleton against the two conditions under which a
matchertext-aware host would contain an injection (that the value sits in a
matcher-delimited slot and is itself valid matchertext) using the passive
\textsc{Verify} check rather than escaping.
We find that the recorded exploit payloads of \textbf{82\%} of payload-backed
injection CVEs are structurally contained by such a slot---read to matcher
balance, with no intervening decoding---and that, projected across all injection
CVEs, at most \textbf{80\%} (a loose upper bound that still includes some
execution sinks) target a matcher-delimitable syntax, the residue being those
sinks and non-matcher-delimited contexts the discipline provably cannot address.
These figures characterize the structure of recorded attack strings under a
matchertext-aware host, not the behavior of a deployed defense: no such host is
implemented or evaluated here.
\end{abstract}
