\begin{abstract}
Injection remains one of the most persistent classes of software
vulnerability, and the \emph{matchertext} discipline has been proposed as a
structural defense: by requiring that the ASCII matcher pairs \verb|()|,
\verb|[]|, and \verb|{}| always match, untrusted input delimited by a matcher
pair cannot break out of its slot without producing an unmatched matcher.
This paper grounds that proposal in evidence.
We assemble a reproducible corpus from thirteen public vulnerability and
proof-of-concept (PoC) sources, anchored on the MITRE CVE List and enriched
with NVD, CISA Vulnrichment, KEV, EPSS, CWE, GitHub/Red~Hat/Debian advisories,
and four PoC databases (Exploit-DB, Nuclei, Metasploit, and PoC-in-GitHub).
From roughly 348k published CVEs we identify \textbf{91{,}760} injection
vulnerabilities and classify each along two axes (weakness family and
injection \emph{syntax} type) using a layered CWE-hierarchy, phrase-rule,
and naive-Bayes classifier.
For the subset carrying an extractable PoC payload, we reduce each attack to a
\emph{syntactic skeleton} and group vulnerabilities by structural shape.
Finally, we test each skeleton against the two conditions under which matchertext
prevents injection (that the value sits in a matcher-delimited slot and is
itself valid matchertext) using the passive \textsc{Verify} check rather than
escaping.
We find that \textbf{82\%} of payload-backed injection CVEs land in a slot their
payload cannot escape, and that this containment splits exactly as the paper's
argument predicts: \textbf{93\%} are neutralized because the payload is valid
matchertext and embeds as inert data, and \textbf{7\%} because it carries an
unmatched matcher and is rejected outright, with the mechanism determined by
whether the host's native delimiter is a nonmatcher (the SQL quote) or a matcher
(the LDAP parenthesis).
Projected across all injection CVEs, \textbf{80\%} target a matchertext-hostable
syntax, the residue being execution/semantic sinks and non-matcher-delimited
contexts the discipline provably cannot address.
\end{abstract}
